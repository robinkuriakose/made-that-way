## Changes from the brief worth knowing about

- **Braille on drive-through ATMs.** The brief's answer was "cheaper to make one keypad than two". The sources don't support that as the reason. US accessibility rules require braille on cash machines with no exception for drive-through ones, and when banks asked for an exemption the Access Board refused, pointing out that blind people use drive-through machines as passengers and in taxis. The question now uses that as the answer, and "one keypad is cheaper" is the half truth option.
- **Cursor tilt.** Alan Kay's account comes from an email he sent to James Hill-Khurana, published on Hill-Khurana's site. Gizmodo traces the low resolution story to a Stack Exchange answer by Bart Gijssels.
- **Chandigarh chair.** Sources agree the name comes from the city. The medium confidence rating is about the design credit, which the V&A and other researchers dispute, so the stem says "often credited to Pierre Jeanneret".
- **Z X C V shortcuts.** Researched as a candidate UI question, then dropped because sources disagree on who chose the keys and why. It was replaced by the ellipsis question.
- **Keypads, split in three.** The old phone keypad question is now three questions in the same family: why calculators put 7 8 9 on top (adding machines kept the most used keys near the hand; medium confidence, because the reasons come from later accounts), why phones put 1 2 3 on top (Bell Labs testing, plus alphabetical letters), and why 0 comes after 9 (the rotary dial sent ten clicks for 0). The rotary dial explains where the 0 sits, but not why 1 2 3 is on top, since calculators also put 0 at the bottom. Questions in the same `group` never appear in the same run.
- **Option lengths.** In the first build the correct option was the longest in 30 of 40 questions. Options were rewritten so length carries no signal: the correct option is never more than two words longer than the longest wrong one, and across the set it's the longest about as often as chance would give. `npm run check:content` enforces this.
